I owned customer education for hCentive, whose cloud platforms ran the health-insurance marketplaces people used to shop for coverage under the Affordable Care Act. The stakes

hCentive built the online marketplaces people used to shop for health insurance under the Affordable Care Act. When the ACA launched, millions of Americans needed to buy coverage online at once, and the legacy government systems weren't ready. hCentive was one of the few companies that stepped in to build the state-level platforms.

The stakes were real. If the software failed during the strict open-enrollment window, people couldn't get insured, states faced public and financial fallout, and carriers could lose the enrollment data that told them who to cover. A broken experience here didn't mean a late delivery. It meant families going without coverage.

The platform only worked if people could actually use it. Brokers, agents, employers, employees, and the state and carrier admins behind them all had to adopt complex software correctly, on deadline. That made customer education part of whether the product succeeded, not an add-on to it.

The strategy

I built customer education into every product launch instead of bolting it on after. I set the standard for how we'd enable customers, partnered directly with Product, Marketing, and Sales, and led a 10-person team to produce the work across five launches. I also built the infrastructure the program would run on, so content stayed findable and current as the platform changed.

The solution

Over two years, the team delivered complete enablement for five major launches: benefits enrollment, broker quoting, the employee benefits portal, the carrier admin portal, and plan management. The mix matched how customers learned. We ran multi-month live ILT campaigns and webinars, built eLearning and playbooks, and added in-product guidance with WalkMe and custom HTML. I built the customer-facing training infrastructure from scratch and partnered with the creative team to keep every piece on brand.
